INTRO title_1 2921 Introduction INTRO paragraph 2934 With the increasing aging population and growing demand for aesthetic dermatology, soft tissue augmentation has become an important clinical approach for restoring facial contours and improving tissue structure. It is widely applied in skin rejuvenation, wrinkle correction, volume restoration, and tissue reconstruction. The fundamental cause of skin aging lies in the progressive reduction of collagen within the extracellular matrix (ECM) of dermal tissues. Collagen, a key structural protein responsible for maintaining the mechanical strength and elasticity of the skin, plays a pivotal role in tissue integrity. Its depletion not only compromises skin firmness but also impairs fibroblast activity and the synthesis of new ECM components. The resulting attenuation of cellâECM mechanotransduction is considered a major contributor to soft tissue degeneration and impaired regenerative capacity, ultimately accelerating volume loss and structural collapse. Therefore, restoring or reconstructing the collagen network and enhancing fibroblast activity have become critical goals in clinical soft tissue augmentation and regenerative research. INTRO paragraph 4083 As a class of injectable materials designed to provide subcutaneous support and restore skin aesthetics, soft tissue fillers are generally categorized into permanent and biodegradable types. Permanent fillers, such as polymethyl methacrylate (PMMA) and silicone, can maintain tissue volume for extended periods but are associated with potential risks of fibrous encapsulation and chronic inflammation due to their poor degradability. Biodegradable fillers, on the other hand, include natural materials (e.g., hyaluronic acid (HA), collagen, and carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC)), and synthetic polymers (e.g., poly-L-lactic acid (PLLA), and polycaprolactone (PCL)). However, natural components often undergo rapid degradation and possess relatively low mechanical strength, limiting their long-term stability. In contrast, synthetic polymer-based fillers can promote fibroblast activation and endogenous collagen neogenesis by eliciting a mild inflammatory response, thereby achieving a sustained bio-stimulatory effect, though occasionally accompanied by adverse reactions or nodular formation. Consequently, the development of next-generation soft tissue fillers with improved tissue integration, controllable degradation, and long-term mechanical compatibility has become a key research focus in both aesthetic and regenerative medicine. INTRO paragraph 5421 Similar to PLLA, Poly (lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA) is an FDA-approved biomaterial that has been widely utilized in bone repair scaffolds and drug delivery systems. However, its application in the field of soft tissue fillers remains limited. The primary limitation lies in the fact that PLGA typically undergoes rapid and complete degradation within 1â3 months. This biodegradation timeline is not aligned with the clinical requirement for soft tissue fillers, which often necessitate a supportive presence for 3â6 months or even longer. For instance, Li et al. developed a PLLA/PLGA microsphere/collagen composite filler, in which the early degradation of PLGA facilitated macrophage infiltration and transforming growth factor-Î² (TGF-Î²) expression, thereby enhancing the formation of type I and type III collagen. Notably, this study leveraged the fast-degrading nature of PLGA to promote early-stage collagen regeneration, with PLLA providing sustained support in the later phase. Therefore, regulating the degradation rate of the PLGA to match the physiological timeline of soft tissue collagen regeneration remains a key scientific challenge that urgently needs to be addressed. INTRO paragraph 6615 Hydroxyapatite (CaHA), as the principal inorganic component of natural hard tissues, has been extensively applied in bone and dental tissue repair, and has also found use in facial soft tissue augmentation. In soft tissue environments, CaHA release Ca2+ ions, activating mechanosensitive transient receptor potential vanilloid 4 (TRPV4) signaling pathways, thereby directly regulating fibroblast proliferation and collagen synthesis. This process is believed to be initiated by the mechanical stimulation of fibroblasts and sustained by Ca2+ release, which drives calcium-mediated biochemical signaling cascades. Liu et al. reported that CaHA achieved in vivo support for up to 6 months and enhanced the fibroblast functionality by triggering minimal adaptive immune responses and enhancing the Calcium Signaling and Motor Protein Signaling pathways. Our previous studies have also demonstrated that CaHA exhibits excellent protein adsorption and cell adhesion properties, providing favorable conditions for endogenous cell recruitment and ECM formation. Furthermore, we have developed a surface modification technique for PLGA using CaHA, which effectively enhances the degradation stability of PLGA. As shown in the Venn diagram, the overlap between PLGA vs Control and CaHA/PLGA vs PLGA is the greatest, with 1834 shared genes. In addition, each comparison group also possesses a certain number of unique genes (Fig. 8B). These results illustrate the overlap and distribution of DEGs among the groups, which is helpful for identifying common or specific regulatory genes and provides data support for further exploring the mechanisms underlying the effects of materials on cellular functions. According to the clustering heatmap and relative gene expression analysis of DEGs, genes that promote tissue repair and ECM deposition, such as Tgfb2, Fgf1, and Pdgfa, were significantly upregulated (Fig. 8C; Fig. S28), which is consistent with previous qRT-PCR results (Fig. 4IâK). In the PLGA group, upregulated biological functions related to the ECM included extracellular matrix, extracellular matrix assembly, and extracellular matrix binding (Fig. 8D). Compared with PLGA, CaHA/PLGA microspheres further enhanced extracellular matrix organization. This can be attributed to CaHA, which activates calcium ion transport and TGF-Î²/SMAD signaling by modulating local Ca2+ concentration, thereby facilitating the regulation of cell behavior and cytokine activity and promoting ECM deposition. According to KEGG enrichment results, PLGA microspheres upregulated ECM-receptor interaction, while pathways related to tissue repair, such as focal adhesion and the TGF-beta signaling pathway, were downregulated (Fig. 8F). In contrast, compared with PLGA microspheres, CaHA/PLGA upregulated the calcium signaling pathway, TGF-beta signaling pathway, chemokine signaling pathway, and downstream PI3K-Akt and MAPK signaling pathways (Fig. 8G). RESULTS paragraph 69565 The GSEA results showed that the calcium signaling pathway, TGF-beta signaling pathway, and chemokine signaling pathway were all significantly enriched and upregulated (Fig. S29). In the calcium signaling pathway, the calmodulin-encoding gene Calm2 and the calcium channel-encoding gene Cacna1g were upregulated, as were their downstream growth factors Tgfa, Vegfa, and Pdgfa (Fig. 8H). Intracellular Ca2+ signaling facilitates the regulation of cell proliferation and differentiation, thereby accelerating tissue repair. In the TGF-beta signaling pathway, multiple genes encoding members of the TGF and SMAD families (e.g., Tgfb1, Tgfb2, Tgfb3, Smad1, Smad3, Smad4, etc.) were upregulated (Fig. 8I). In soft tissue, TGF-Î² signaling is a key driver of tissue remodeling and can influence the quality of soft tissue reconstruction by promoting fibroblast transformation and type I collagen expression. In the chemokine signaling pathway, chemokines or receptors such as C-C motif chemokine ligand 2 (Ccl2), C-X-C motif chemokine ligand 12 (Cxcl12), and C-C chemokine receptor type 7 (Ccr7) were upregulated (Fig. 8J). Protein expression of TGF-Î²1, SMAD3, CACNA1G, CALM, CXCL12, and CCR7, which are associated with the calcium, TGF, and chemokine signaling pathways, was further confirmed by Western blot analysis (Fig. S30A). Meanwhile, due to the influence of the surface structure and Ca2+, CaHA/PLGA microspheres activated the MAPK, PI3K-AKT, and JAK-STAT pathways by upregulating key genes and proteins such as ERK1/2, PIK3CA, AKT1, JAK2, and JAK3 through the chemokine signaling pathway (Fig. 8J; Fig. S30B). In addition, the Ca2+ released from the CaHA/PLGA microspheres upregulated the calcium channel protein CACNA1G and the calcium-binding protein CALM, thereby indirectly promoting MAPK pathway signal transduction. Collectively, through these mechanisms, CaHA/PLGA microspheres promote ADSCs to establish a microenvironment favorable for tissue reconstruction. Moreover, in KEGG enrichment analysis, the pathways influenced by CaHA/PLGA were further enhanced (Fig. S33). CONCL title_1 73923 Conclusion CONCL paragraph 73934 In this study, a simple system was used to develop mossy-textured CaHA-modified PLGA microspheres for soft tissue augmentation, referred to as CaHA/PLGA.
